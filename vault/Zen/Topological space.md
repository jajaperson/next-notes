# Topological space

Abstractly, a **topological space** $(X, \mathcal T)$ consists of a set $X$
and a collection of subsets $\mathcal T \sube 2^{X}$
such that[^br] #m/def/topology

1. $\mathcal T$ contains at least $\0$ and $X$.
1. Any finite or infinite union of subsets in $\mathcal T$ is also in $\mathcal T$.
1. Any finite intersection of subsets in $\mathcal T$ is also in $\mathcal T$.

where $\mathcal T$ is called a **topology** on $X$,
and is said to contain **open subsets** of $X$.
A subset of $X$ is called **closed** iff its compliment is open.
Thus, in any topological space $(X, \mathcal{T})$ the subsets $X$ and $\0$ are **clopen**^\[Simultaneously open and closed.\].

On any set $X$ we can easily form the [Discrete topology](Discrete%20topology.md) $2^X$ (every set is **clopen**)
and the [Trivial topology](Trivial%20topology.md) $\{ \0, X \}$.

Two topologies on the same space $X$ can be compared in terms of [Coarseness and fineness of topologies](Coarseness%20and%20fineness%20of%20topologies.md).

A topology can be generated by a [Topological basis](Topological%20basis.md).

[^br]: 2020, [Topology: A categorical approach](../Sources/@bradleyTopologyCategoricalApproach2020.md), §0.1, p. 1
